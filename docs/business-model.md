# LeadScale Systems — Business Model, One Page

Brisbane, SEQ · Internal alignment · v0.1 · 10 Aug 2026

**STATUS: PRE-VERTICAL — trade not yet chosen**

---

## 1 · What we are

We are the trade that installs and runs the back-office systems a trade business already half-owns — and we are accountable for one number.

Not a software company. Not an AI company. Simpro, ServiceTitan, AroFlo, Tradify, ServiceM8 and Fergus all ship native AI already. The tool is not the scarce thing. The scarce thing is someone who walks in, measures what's actually leaking, configures it correctly for this business, and stays on the hook for the result. Bunnings sells the gear; the sparky does the job. We're the sparky.

## 2 · Phase 0 — Vertical discovery (not started)

We have not chosen a trade and will not build product until we have. Candidates: HVAC · Roofing · Plumbing · Electrical.

## 3 · The ladder

**1. Diagnostic** — 2 hrs on site. We measure admin hours/week, inbound response time, quote→job conversion, time-to-invoice. They keep a one-page report with a dollar figure, hire us or not. Does four jobs: it's the pitch, the baseline, the qualifier, and the research.

**2. Install** — Fixed-scope build of our standard module against the one bottleneck. Same system every time, configured — never rebuilt.

**3. Retainer** — We own the number. Monthly report against the baseline. This is the real business; the install is the entry fee.

Year-one target: 5 retained clients in one trade. Not 20 clients across four.

## 5 · How we go to market

- **Round 1** — 6–8 structured operator conversations per trade (~30 total). Rank by bottleneck consistency, not by market size.
- **Round 2** — 20 deep conversations in the top-ranked trade only.
- **Selection rule** — the same bottleneck must recur in ≥12 of 20. If it doesn't, the vertical is wrong — change trade, not approach.
- We are researching, not selling. No pitch, no price, no product talk in Phase 0.
- **Sell the number, never the technology.** "You're losing roughly $X a year and here's how I'd measure it" — AI is never mentioned unless asked, then answered plainly and dropped.
- **Referral density is the entire plan.** Trades verify each other. One operator vouching for us beats any funnel.
- **Physical presence, 12 months not 12 weeks.** Two or three associations, every month, sponsoring rather than exhibiting. Present data, never the service.
- **Publish the benchmark.** After ~25 diagnostics we own numbers nobody else has: what response time and quote conversion actually look like across SEQ operators in one trade. Annual report = authority, sales tool and moat in one asset.
- **Three case studies with before/after figures.** First three clients are the marketing budget — price accordingly, negotiate publishing rights up front.
- **Not doing:** paid ads, AI-branded content, futurist thought leadership. All three signal the exact thing we're trying not to be.

## 6 · What compounds (the longevity bet)

Assume that in three years native AI makes installation trivial. Four things survive that:

- **Accountability, not software.** A retainer where we own a metric can't be shipped in a product release. Someone still has to care every week.
- **The benchmark dataset.** Exists only because we insist on baseline capture. Gets harder to replicate with every audit.
- **Depth in one trade, one city.** Defensible. Breadth across four is not.
- **The referral graph.** Platforms can buy distribution; they cannot buy this.

Failure mode to avoid: becoming a thin configuration layer on someone else's product. That gets squeezed to zero.

## 7 · Rules we don't break

- **NO custom work.** Every bespoke request in year one is a client we keep and a business we never get.
- **NO install without a captured baseline.** Skip it and we can't defend a renewal or write a case study.
- **NO second trade** until the first has 5 retained clients.
- **NO leading with AI. Ever.**

## 8 · Go / kill gates

1. **Vertical locked** — one bottleneck recurs in ≥12/20. Fail → change trade.
2. **Demand proven** — 3 operators grant a 2-hour on-site diagnostic. Fail → the offer is wrong.
3. **Value proven** — first paid install shows a measured delta vs baseline. Fail → the module is wrong.
4. **Distribution proven** — 2 of first 5 clients arrive by referral. Fail → we're not present enough.

**Open questions:** which trade · what the recurring bottleneck actually is · whether the diagnostic should be paid · what number we're willing to guarantee
